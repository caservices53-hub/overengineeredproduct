    Questions

    What buyers ask before the first call.

    Why design the whole system before building anything?
    Because the expensive mistakes are made in the first fortnight and discovered in the sixth month. Agreeing the data model, the boundaries between services and the ways the system can fail costs a few weeks. Unpicking them after launch costs a rewrite.
    How do you work across time zones?
    As a matter of routine. We keep a guaranteed overlap with your working day — whether you are in London, New York, Dubai or Singapore — and everything that matters is written down, so progress never waits on a call. Weekly previews and written notes mean you can review whenever suits you.
    Who owns the code, the designs and the infrastructure?
    You do, entirely, from the first commit. We work in your repository and your cloud accounts where possible. Source, design files, credentials and documentation are all yours, with no licence conditions and no obligation to keep us as host or maintainer.
    What does a programme cost, and how long does it take?
    A marketing site typically takes five to seven weeks; an enterprise platform, four to nine months, depending on integrations and the role of AI. The estimator above gives a rough calendar in ten seconds. You receive a fixed price in writing after paid discovery, before any build begins, so the figure cannot move on you mid-programme.
    Can you work alongside our in-house engineering team?
    Yes — that is what the embedded model is for. We join your rituals, your issue tracker and your branching strategy, and work to your review standards. We can also own a single workstream, such as the platform core or the AI layer, while your team keeps the rest.
    How do you handle NDAs, intellectual property and data protection?
    We sign your NDA and master services agreement before discovery; we do not insist on our own paperwork. Intellectual property assigns to you on payment. For anything touching personal data we work to UK GDPR and EU GDPR principles: data minimisation, named sub-processors, and no production data on local machines.
